Curatorial statement

Every change has a geography.

Before a change becomes a decision or an event, it is a terrain. It has its own distances, borders and directions. These places appear on no map. We recognise them by distance, by what has dissolved and by what still holds.

The Geography of Becoming brings together Inessa Garder and Anna Katsur, two artists who paint landscape as an inner state. Garder paints water and the ways we cross it: rivers, bridges and reflections. Katsur paints architecture assembled from memory, with empty roads leading to distant horizons. In both practices, a place is the shape that transformation takes.

About the artists
b. 1988

Inessa Garder

Inessa Garder is a visual artist with a background in architecture. She studied architecture in Milan and continued her education at Central Saint Martins, University of the Arts London. She worked as an architect in Milan, Rome and London, and this research-led, large-scale approach became the foundation of her painting.

From 2019 to 2022 she lived in Northern India, painting and exhibiting at international art and film festivals. From 2022 to 2024 she lived and worked in the Hackney Wick warehouse community in London, creating immersive community-based projects. Her current practice centres on multi-layered liquid oil painting.

Anna Katsur

Anna Katsur is an artist working in oil painting. Her practice addresses philosophical and socio-political questions of the twenty-first century. She explores control and chance, authorship and intuition, and the role of memory in how a place is seen and remembered.
