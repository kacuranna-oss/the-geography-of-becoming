Curatorial statement

Every change has a geography.

We like to think of our lives as stories we write ourselves. We make decisions, imagine futures, choose where to go and what to leave behind.

But the world has a curious habit of participating.

A beautiful future glows beyond a window, making the present difficult to inhabit. A bridge stretches across a river whose currents have their own direction. A door knocker appears where there is no door. A familiar tower stands in unfamiliar waters. Human figures emerge and dissolve, as though even the traveller herself cannot remain unchanged.

The Geography of Becoming brings together six women artists exploring the relationship between the traveller and her path: how we shape it, how it shapes us, and what remains with us along the way.

The exhibition unfolds through a series of stations, without a prescribed order or a final destination. Visitors may enter at any point, follow different routes and return to places they have already encountered.

Perhaps our lives unfold in much the same way. We revisit old questions from new perspectives. A place we have left continues to live within us. An experience changes the meaning of something that happened years before. We carry traces of everywhere we have been, even as we move towards somewhere unknown.

But these traces do more than remind us where we have been. They change what we are able to see and who we are. A path that once seemed closed may reveal an opening. A destination that once felt essential may no longer matter. We may return to exactly the same place and find ourselves in an entirely different landscape.

The journey changes the traveller. And the traveller, changed, discovers a different geography.

    Karina Mukhametkulova is a London-based artist working in oil, bringing real objects and materials into saturated painted spaces.

    Her work begins with the sense that the ordinary has another layer, always present and easy to miss. A space changes whoever is inside it; whoever notices it changes the space in return. She is drawn to that moment of noticing – something that, once seen, cannot be unseen – and to the stillness of a moment already slipping away. Artworks by station

In the gallery the works hang in seven stations along one corridor. There is no fixed route: start anywhere, follow the stations in any order and come back to the ones you have already seen.

01 Before the Road02 The Threshold03 Unfamiliar Waters04 Companions05 Somewhere Along the Way06 Expected07 The Traveller, Changed

01

Station 1 of 7

Before the Road

A path often begins long before anyone sets out on it. It shows first as a shift in the light, or a restlessness that has no object yet, a sense that the present arrangement of things will not hold much longer. The traveller is still standing still, and the road is already under way somewhere ahead of her.

Keep this water in mind. The same river waits at the end of the corridor.

02

Station 2 of 7

The Threshold

This painting belongs with the one on the opposite wall. Here, a window opens onto a landscape lit so brightly that the room you stand in begins to feel provisional. Across the corridor, doors open onto further doors with no final room. Between them, the future is something you can already picture but cannot yet enter, and picturing it changes what it feels like to stay where you are.

03

Station 3 of 7

Unfamiliar Waters

Once you are on the way, the map you set out with stops matching the ground. Familiar landmarks appear in unfamiliar settings, and colours that once held their shape begin to drift. On the wall behind you, the temperature rises. Change rarely arrives all at once. It accumulates by degrees, and by the time we notice it, we have usually been living inside it for some while.

04

Station 4 of 7

Companions

We tend to imagine the traveller alone, but most roads are walked beside someone. People pass through us and leave something behind, as the figures on this wall pass into one another. On the opposite wall, two people are crossing a bridge together. It is often in someone else’s company that we first see how far we have already come.

05

Station 5 of 7

Somewhere Along the Way

Some places ask us to stay. A room keeps space ready for something to take root, and the figure leaving it walks towards the sea anyway. Further on, you find yourself inside a landscape that seems complete, warm and still, with a fault line running through it. Arrival and rest are easily mistaken for each other. Whether this is the end of the road or a place to gather strength, the painting does not say.

06

Station 6 of 7

Expected

Not every part of a journey involves movement. Some of it happens in place: in noticing what was easy to pass by, or in deciding whether to answer. Here there is a hand ready to knock and no door to knock on. As the path goes on it grows subtler, and asks for attention more than distance. What it means to be expected depends on which side of the door you imagine yourself standing.

07

Station 7 of 7

The Traveller, Changed

The river from the entrance returns here, at dusk and in violet: the same place, seen after the whole corridor. To the left, a piece of lace that was once a curtain, then a pillowcase, now spills over the edge of a canvas. Each use changed it, and the lace has kept something of each. People change in a similar way. What we pass through settles in us and alters what we are able to see. In the corner hangs a work that visitors take apart, piece by piece, and carry out with them.

On your way back, look at the works again. You will see them in reverse order, and probably differently.
